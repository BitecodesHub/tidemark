## Executive summary
- I set out to build a fast, general "AI operating system". Research showed that this idea has no defensible novelty, so I dropped it.
- I chose a narrower project, Tidemark: a headless, signed Fedora bootc image in which what an agent session has read decides where that session's data may go next, including which model sees it.
- The research has real limits: the web-search budget ran out early, and nearly all willingness-to-pay figures come from vendors.
- The next three weeks test the riskiest assumptions first: a kernel spike on stock Fedora 45 and 8 to 10 observational interviews. On Sunday 8 November I will publish a written go or no-go decision.

## Where this started
The original goal was an operating system tuned for the fastest possible AI performance: bundled inference engines, training tools, hardware adapters and a desktop. Before writing any code, I asked for deep research into the problems that AI companies and ordinary companies actually face, and into whether a bootable OS was the right vehicle at all.

The answer to the original goal was no. NVIDIA, Bluefin, Omarchy and Ollama already ship equivalent stacks for free. Performance work also cannot be tested on my development machine, an Apple M1 Pro with 16 GB.

## How the research was done
- Three AI research workflows ran 81 agents in total. One covered 22 market and technical areas plus 6 gap-fill areas, with fact-checking and a synthesis. One covered bootable-OS feasibility in 5 areas, with fact-checking. One had 5 independent concept architects, 3 judges, a synthesis, 3 red-team reviewers and a final revision.
- The evidence rests on about 1,500 direct reads of primary sources: project documentation, repositories, kernel sources, papers and vendor reports.
- The full research is in the repository under `docs/research/`. Treat it as research input, not as established fact.

## What the research found
**Already commoditised (September to October 2026), so I integrate rather than rebuild:**
- Agent sandboxing: Microsoft Execution Containers, NVIDIA OpenShell, Anthropic sandbox-runtime, Docker Sandboxes and GitHub Copilot sandboxing.
- Agent identity and inventory: Microsoft Agent 365, AWS AgentCore and Google Agent Identity.
- Protocols: MCP and A2A, now held by the Agentic AI Foundation.

**Severe and still unsolved (research atlas scores):**
- Data leaving through destinations that are allowed (240). Containment blocks unknown hosts, but secrets still leave through allowlisted ones.
- The verification bottleneck (240). One reported figure says users approve about 93% of Claude Code permission prompts, with no base disclosed. Omarchy launches 11 of its 15 agents with prompts disabled.
- Agent supply chain (192), irreversible actions (180) and forgeable logs (144).

**Earlier "AI OS" attempts** either failed or were absorbed: Humane, Rabbit, the OpenAI Atlas browser, Google Mariner and the AIOS research kernel.

## The decision: Tidemark
**The claim, as a v0.1 preview.** What an agent session has read decides where its data may go next, including which model sees it. The design targets unmodified agents from different vendors (Hermes Agent and OpenCode first) and every MCP server, hook and subprocess they start. Anyone should be able to check the claim with a canary they generate on their own machine and grep for in logs kept outside the VM.

**How it is meant to work (designed, not built):**
- The kernel records each labelled read before the read returns, using sleepable BPF LSM programs.
- Every exit re-checks the session's current label before it forwards each chunk, so a tunnel opened before the read is cut.
- The model call is treated as an exit: a labelled session goes only to backends cleared for its label, by default the local model, and otherwise fails closed with a reason.
- What a labelled session writes carries its label forward when a human commits it, and agent memory is partitioned by label.
- A daemon the agent cannot reach signs receipts of every decision.

**What is not new.** The high-water mark dates to ADEPT-50 in 1969. SELinux MLS, Solaris Trusted Extensions, HiStar, Flume, ActPlane, CaMeL, FIDES, OpenShell, nono and agentsh all cover parts of this ground. The new piece, as far as public READMEs showed on 16 October 2026, is a narrow combination: floating labels on the process trees of unmodified agents from different vendors, the model API as a labelled exit, per-chunk re-checks, label-carrying writes and label-partitioned agent memory, and all of it shipped as a reproducible image with a demo strangers can check.

**What Tidemark will not claim:** prevention of prompt injection; resistance to kernel or root compromise; protection against covert or timing channels to destinations the user cleared; protection against misuse by those destinations; protection of unlabelled data; content inspection; holding or undoing external side effects in v0.1; or third-party proof against the machine's owner.

**Why Tidemark and not the top-scoring concept.** A naming mismatch split the judges' scores. Once corrected, the security-first "Escrow" concept scored 122 and Tidemark 116. I chose Tidemark because it absorbs Escrow's core within a scope that one part-time maintainer can deliver.

**First users.** Homelab, Proxmox and dedicated-VM users who run coding or research agents over local code, notes and secrets, mixing a local model with cheap cloud models. Agent-security researchers are the second audience, through a standalone benchmark.

## Limitations of this research
Please read these before quoting any number from the research.
- **The web-search budget ran out early.** Most evidence comes from direct reads of primary sources. Some areas therefore have thinner coverage than a full search would give.
- **Willingness-to-pay figures are vendor-sponsored.** No buyer interviews exist yet. I do not need willingness to pay for an open-source v0.1, but I will not cite those figures as evidence of demand.
- **The research was produced by AI agents.** Independent agents fact-checked each brief, but errors remain possible. I verify every fact the build depends on against primary sources before relying on it.
- **The judges' scoring had a naming error**, described above.
- **The time estimates assume strong systems experience plus AI-assisted coding.** I review every security-critical diff myself, and I will recruit two or three named reviewers before launch.

## Facts checked today (16 October 2026)
- Linux kernel sources: Landlock ABI 9 arrives in Linux 7.1 and ABI 10 in 7.2. The `file_open` and `file_receive` hooks are in the sleepable BPF LSM hook set. The kfuncs `bpf_get_file_xattr`, `bpf_path_d_path` and `bpf_real_data_inode` are in `fs/bpf_fs_kfuncs.c`.
- **Correction to the plan:** `bpf_real_data_inode`, which the overlay-safe label cache depends on, first appears in Linux 7.2, not 7.1. Fedora 44 and Fedora 45 both ship 7.2 kernels today (Fedora 45 had 7.2.9 in stable on 6 October 2026), so the Phase 0 spike can use it. I propose 7.2 as the kernel floor for v0.1, as the draft labeller decision record does; the decision is still open and will be settled there after the spike. That record also describes a design that does not depend on the kfunc, reading the xattr on every open with no cache; if the spike selects it, the floor may be revisited.
- The `quay.io/fedora/fedora-bootc:45` tag is rebuilt frequently, so the image is pinned by digest, and the digest and its date are recorded in the base-image decision record.
- Hermes Agent (MIT) has about 254,000 GitHub stars and OpenClaw (MIT) about 392,000, so both are widely starred. OpenClaw nevertheless stays outside the v0.1 claims and gets its profile in Phase 6; the interviews will show whether that order is right.

The phase ends in two kill tests, decided on **Sunday 8 November 2026**:
1. **Kernel.** If label raising is unreliable on stock Fedora 45 on either architecture, or through overlayfs, and the fallback misses reads by child processes or through mmap, I drop the kernel-label claim. I then either ship the gateway, broker and receipts as an audit tool, or stop. This is the concept document's wording. The summary in the approved plan leaves out the fallback condition, so I will record which wording governs before the spike runs. If the fallback condition applies, only a test of the fallback can satisfy it, not a written analysis.
2. **Users.** If fewer than 3 interview participants run agents over local private files or secrets on a machine or VM they control, or if a majority would abandon a session rather than accept denied public-web access after it reads private data, I re-scope to a benchmark-first project for security researchers before building Phase 1.
